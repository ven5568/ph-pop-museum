THE HINGE

Philippine popular culture in 2000-2010 was a transitional "hinge" period between broadcast-centered culture and the increasingly networked culture of the 2010s.

"Memories. Moments. Movements. We Lived It."

Room 06 — Patterns, Continuity & Change

Exhibition Purpose: What continued from earlier decades, what shifted, and how the 2000s became a bridge to the 2010s.

Three Recurring Patterns:

  • Youth subcultures, identity, and cultural continuity.
  • Transnational media consumption and local indigenization.
  • Commercialization of celebrity culture and performance.

Four Major Changes:

  • Youth identity moved partly online through Friendster and texting.
  • Foreign influences diversified through East Asian dramas and anime.
  • Media access became more self-directed through internet cafés.
  • Reality television created additional pathways to fame.

The Hinge Timeline

Tracing the shift from broadcast to network.

2000 - 2002

The Peak of Y2K. Low-rise jeans, chunky shoes, and the rise of SMS texting. Internet cafés begin to pop up across the metro.

2003 - 2006

The OPM Rock Renaissance hits mainstream radio. Friendster explodes, creating the "Top 8" culture. Asianovelas dominate prime-time TV.

2007 - 2010

Competitive gaming (DotA, CS 1.6) fills internet cafés. Reality talent shows create overnight celebrities. The shift toward modern social media begins.
